# Hilltide: project brief

## Name
Hilltide
Tagline: Rwanda's thousand hills, alive.

Hilltide: Rwanda’s thousand hills, alive.

It carries all three acts in two syllables:

Hill is the land: Act 1.
Tide is water rising and flowing: Act 3, the rain.
Tide also means time, as in “eventide” or “the tide of history”: Act 2, the city growing year by year.

## What it is
A single cinematic web experience that runs live in the browser, on laptops and
phones. It tells the story of Kigali in three acts. Nothing is pre-recorded:
everything is computed in real time from real, open data.

- Act 1, The Land: the page opens on Rwanda seen from space, in live 3D. As the
  visitor scrolls, the camera dives across the real thousand hills (satellite
  elevation data) and settles over Kigali. Real terrain, real shadows, and the
  real sun position for the current time in Kigali.
- Act 2, Kigali Builds Itself (2016 to 2023): a time slider. Dragging it makes
  the city rise out of the hills, building by building, year by year, with live
  counts ("+X buildings since 2016"). Data: Google Open Buildings 2.5D Temporal
  (annual building presence and heights) and Open Buildings footprints.
- Act 3, Make It Rain: a rainfall slider. Rain falls on the real terrain; a
  water simulation running on the visitor's graphics chip sends water down the
  real hills into streams and valleys, pooling where Kigali really floods.
- How We Know It's Real: measured validation results, generated from the actual
  test runs (flow paths against mapped rivers, pooling against documented flood
  hotspots, growth against known developments, measured frame rates).
- Data and Credits: every source, license and required attribution.

## Why it exists
1. A calling card: proof of excellence in 3D graphics, GPU simulation, data
   engineering and design, combined in one product. Built to be shared on
   social media and taken seriously by engineers.
2. Public good: helps people see how their city grew and where water goes.
3. Foundation: the terrain and water engine can later grow into a serious
   flood-risk product, and it showcases the 3D and terrain skills behind Brink.

## Audience
Social media viewers (the 15-second clips), engineers and technical recruiters,
potential partners (city planners, insurers, universities), and Kigali residents.

## Principles
- Real, not fake: every number shown is computed, every claim validated.
- Honest limits stated on the page: this is a visualization, not a flood
  warning; terrain cells are about 30 m; building heights are estimates from
  satellite imagery; the building data ends in 2023.
- Beautiful and fast on ordinary devices, not only on gaming PCs.
- Distinctive design: it should feel like a documentary, not a dashboard or a
  template.

## Success
- A visitor says "how is this running in my browser?" within 10 seconds.
- Three shareable vertical clips, one per act, exported from the site itself.
- Validation results published on the page, with methods.
- Works on a mid-range Android phone.
